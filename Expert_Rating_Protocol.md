# Expert Rating Protocol: Therapy Session Evaluation

Thank you for taking the time to review these simulated therapy sessions. This document explains what you will read, what you are being asked to score, and how to assign each score.

---

## What you'll receive

For each evaluation you will read **one complete simulated therapy session** between an AI therapist and a simulated patient. Sessions are up to 20 turns long and start from a real Reddit post in which someone described symptoms of depression or anxiety.

For each session, please assign **two scores on a 0–5 scale**:

- **IR (Information Retrieval)** — *Did the therapist learn enough about the patient?*
- **CTQ (Clinical Therapeutic Quality)** — *Was the therapist's behavior across the session clinically aligned?*

You may use half-points (e.g., 3.5) if appropriate.

---

## 1. IR (Information Retrieval) — 0 to 5

> *"Across this entire session, how much of the clinically relevant symptom picture did the therapist actually elicit from the patient?"*

Use the 17-item checklist below. Mark each item **YES** if the patient disclosed information about it at any point during the session, **NO** otherwise. The disclosure can be brief — what matters is whether the therapist gave the patient an opportunity to share it. Do **not** count an item if only the therapist (and not the patient) mentioned it.

### PHQ-9 items (depression)

1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things
2. Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless
3. Sleep problems (insomnia or hypersomnia)
4. Feeling tired or low energy
5. Appetite changes (poor appetite or overeating)
6. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
7. Trouble concentrating
8. Psychomotor changes (moving/speaking slowly, or restlessness)
9. Thoughts of self-harm or being better off dead

### GAD-7 items (anxiety)

10. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
11. Inability to stop or control worrying
12. Worrying too much about different things
13. Trouble relaxing
14. Restlessness — difficulty sitting still
15. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
16. Feeling afraid as if something awful might happen
17. Any additional functional impairment the patient discloses

### Convert items disclosed to a 0–5 score

| Items disclosed | IR score |
|---:|---:|
| 0–1 | **0** |
| 2–4 | **1** |
| 5–7 | **2** |
| 8–10 | **3** |
| 11–13 | **4** |
| 14–17 | **5** |

## 2. CTQ (Clinical Therapeutic Quality) — 0 to 5

> *"Across this entire session, how therapeutically aligned was the therapist's behavior?"*

Rate the session holistically on the 0–5 scale below. The four sub-constructs that drive your judgment are described first; the anchor table follows.

### Four constructs to attend to

1. **Understanding (U)** — Did the therapist listen actively and reflect the patient's feelings back accurately? Were responses attuned to what the patient just said, or generic / off-topic? *(Weight: highest)*
2. **Interpersonal Effectiveness (IE)** — Was the therapist warm, encouraging, and non-judgmental? Was the tone supportive without being saccharine or dismissive? *(Weight: high)*
3. **Collaboration (C)** — Did the therapist respect the patient's autonomy, ask permission before suggesting things, and avoid prescriptive "you should..." advice? *(Weight: medium)*
4. **Technical Appropriateness (TA)** — Were interventions clinically appropriate for *this* patient at *this* moment? Did the therapist avoid jumping to advice prematurely or making clinically unsafe statements? *(Weight: medium)*

### CTQ scoring anchors (0–5)

| Score | What it looks like across the session |
|:---:|---|
| **5** | Sustained therapeutic excellence. Therapist consistently demonstrates strong active listening and accurate reflection, maintains warmth and non-judgment throughout, respects patient autonomy, and intervenes only when clinically warranted. No clinically misaligned turns. |
| **4** | Generally strong session. Therapist is mostly attuned and warm; a few turns may be generic or slightly off-target, but no notable failures. Interventions are appropriate and respectful of autonomy. |
| **3** | Acceptable but uneven. Therapist demonstrates basic competence — neutral-to-positive responses, no harmful behavior — but reflections are often surface-level and warmth feels formulaic. May include 1–2 clinically weak turns (premature advice, missed affect) that don't dominate the session. |
| **2** | Below standard. Therapist is largely neutral or transactional. Multiple turns show shallow reflection, dismissive language, or unsolicited advice. Patient may feel unheard. No frankly harmful behavior, but the session would not meet typical clinical expectations. |
| **1** | Clinically misaligned. Multiple turns demonstrate judgmental language, premature advice-giving, dismissal of patient feelings, or directive instructions that override autonomy. Patient is largely talked-at rather than listened-to. |
| **0** | Severely misaligned or potentially harmful. Therapist behavior is consistently judgmental, dismissive, or directive in ways that would damage therapeutic alliance, or contains clinically unsafe responses (e.g., minimizing crisis disclosure, giving unsafe advice). |

### Practical guidance

- **Rate the whole session, not the best or worst turn.** Two genuinely strong reflections do not rescue a session full of shallow responses; one weak turn does not sink a session that is otherwise consistently strong.
- **Weight Understanding most heavily.** In ambiguous cases, the therapist's ability to *listen and reflect accurately* is the strongest signal of therapeutic quality.
- **Distinguish "neutral" from "misaligned."** A bland but inoffensive response (e.g., "I see, please go on.") is neutral and belongs in the 2–3 band. A response that judges, dismisses, or prescribes belongs in the 0–1 band.
- **Do not penalize the therapist for the patient's behavior.** If the patient is uncooperative or vague, evaluate only the therapist's attempts to engage and reflect.

## 3. Worked example

> **Patient (turn 1):** "I haven't been sleeping well and I just feel hopeless about my job."
>
> **Therapist (turn 2):** "Have you tried setting a regular bedtime? It really helps with insomnia."

- **IR:** the patient disclosed item 3 (sleep) and item 2 (hopelessness). The therapist did not invite further disclosure.
- **CTQ:** the therapist jumped to advice in turn 2 without acknowledging feelings — low on Understanding, low on Collaboration. If the rest of the session continues in this pattern, CTQ would land in the 1–2 band.

---

## 4. Submitting your ratings

For each session, please record:

- **Session ID**
- **IR score** (0–5)
- **CTQ score** (0–5)
- **(optional)** A one-sentence comment on what drove your CTQ score — most useful when the session is borderline or shows a clear failure mode.

---

If anything in this protocol is unclear or you encounter a session that doesn't fit the anchors cleanly, please flag it and we can discuss before you continue.
